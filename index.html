01 Profile

Rigor first, then the story.

I like problems where the math has to hold up and the answer still has to make sense to someone who will never see the math.

That started in a Columbia lab, where a year of metabolomics data taught me how much careful analysis it takes to say one true thing. Now I apply it to people: how players use a sports app, and how small-business owners find their way into a program built to help them grow.

I'm building toward a career in finance, where the same habits matter most: be precise, be skeptical of a clean-looking number, and explain it clearly.

  • i. Quantitative analysis
  • ii. Data storytelling
  • iii. Finance & consulting

02 Experience

Where I've been doing the work.

SwingVision

Data Analyst · 2026 — Now

SwingVision records every shot a player hits. My job is to work out what more than a billion of those shots, from half a million players, say about the people hitting them, and to turn that into marketing and product decisions.

  • Find the signalSampling and exploratory work across ratings, demographics, subscription tiers, and improvement curves to spot where players engage and where they drift off.
  • Make it usableA marketing insights dashboard that managers use to decide which content to make and which segments to target.
  • Train the models4,700+ human-checked annotations that feed the computer-vision models behind the app.

CU

Columbia University

Researcher · 2024 — 2025

My first serious dataset: metabolomics from zebrafish bred to model Batten disease, a rare neurodegenerative disorder, at the Mailman School of Public Health.
